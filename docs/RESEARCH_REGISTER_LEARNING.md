# Learning from demonstrations and public model weights

The strongest clue about the remaining simulated grasp failures is the initial cube position. All 11 fresh starts within the convex hull of the 32 training positions produced terminal holds with each of four controllers. Of nine outside starts, the action-chunking transformer held four, diffusion held seven, radial basis interpolation held nine and inverse kinematics held seven. At seed 940218 the camera differed from the reset centre by only 1.41 mm, but the nearest movement demonstration started 18.65 mm away. Both learned policies failed there while both geometric methods held the cube. On the native command path in simulation the interpolator lost terminal hold in three of 20 scenes, including two which rose above 50 mm. Coverage motivates a separate controlled comparison of broadened simulated starts at a fixed demonstration budget. The existing deployment protocol uses measured physical points and independently verified end-of-trial retention. **Does selecting placements improve grasping at a fixed demonstration budget?** We froze 128 candidate cube positions before observing any expert outcomes. Both acquisition rules used the same privileged MuJoCo teacher and accepted 32/32 successful attempts each. Eight demonstrations were shared. Random selection left a maximum 23.56 mm gap between a candidate and its nearest demonstration. Farthest-first selection reduced it to 11.38 mm. The policies used the same frozen RGB localizer, SciPy thin-plate interpolator, native arm guards and 360-command limit on 20 new starts. Random selection held 8/20 and farthest-first held 10/20. Five scenes favoured farthest-first, three favoured random, five succeeded under both and seven failed under both (exact paired two-sided *p* = 0.727). Refusing targets outside each training hull gave 7/20 and 9/20 respectively. Nine of the ten farthest-first failures lifted the cube by more than 50 mm, but none met even the simulator's instantaneous grasp criterion. One pool and 20 paired scenes give limited evidence of a general task-success gain. The original collector stopped on a phase-name check before evaluation; the corrected collector retained the same placement orders and criteria. Physical trials were zero. ## Wrist-camera keypoint localization and terminal grasp

The original pretrained ResNet-18 regressed base-frame XY directly from one wrist image. In opened scene 976115 it missed a fully visible cube by 39.30 mm. We adapted integral keypoint regression to this camera. The network predicts a spatial distribution for the projected cube centre. A planar map fitted to the training set and an image-conditioned residual convert pixels to metres. Both localizers used the same 1,024 simulated training images and ImageNet initialization. The new model received additional synthetic pixel labels on those images. Its 960 optimizer updates used seed 1000. Validation p90 across 128 separate images selected epoch 28. The input to the saved model is one RGB image from the fixed look pose. [Integral Human Pose Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08229) · [Torchvision backbone](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/stable/models/generated/torchvision.models.resnet18.html) · [Original trainer](../scripts/recorded_start_visual_pick.py) · [Keypoint model and portable inference](../scripts/grasp_study/wrist_heatmap/model.py) · [Protocol](results/conditioned_grasp_v1/wrist-heatmap/protocol.json) · [Training and validation image shards with pixel labels](http://192.168.0.168:8030/simlab-media/reports/wrist-cube-heatmap-v1/image-splits.zip)

On 48 prospectively assigned fresh MuJoCo starts, median error fell from 3.38 to 2.75 mm and p90 from 6.28 to 5.74 mm. The maximum fell from 16.43 to 9.10 mm. Errors above 10 mm fell from one to zero. Neither model exceeded 20 mm on this block. On the opened diagnostic scene the new model's error was 1.80 mm. End-to-end retention showed a different result. With identical guarded native actuation and the original 32-demonstration motor library, unrestricted simulation produced 29/48 terminal holds for direct regression and 27/48 for the keypoint model. Eight starts improved, ten worsened (paired exact two-sided *p* = 0.815). The same full-visibility and predicted motor-support checks admitted the same 15 scenes under both models. ## Episode-calibrated visual failure alarms

SAFE trains a probe on a policy's activations to estimate failure. FIPER combines uncertainty in sampled actions with a visual novelty signal. Explorer's frozen RGB–RBF controller takes deterministic actions. We adapted the first idea using two time points from the wrist camera, frozen visual features and a logistic failure score. A normalized template tracker provided the simple comparison. Forty simulated episodes supplied terminal labels. Twenty-four separate episodes fixed thresholds using the 12th of 14 successful calibration-episode scores sorted in ascending order, targeting at most one alarm per episode and nominal false-alarm risk of 20% on successes. The original strict runner stopped before producing a test endpoint when two renders disagreed. Confirmation preserved the exact model, thresholds and assigned seeds and replayed each shared camera exposure only at the matching complete physics state and camera optics. [SAFE](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/392d0d05e2f514063e6ce6f8b370834c-Abstract-Conference.html) · [FIPER](https://tum-lsy.github.io/fiper_website/) · [Frozen design](results/conditioned_grasp_v1/failure-watch/protocol.json) · [Confirmation amendment](results/conditioned_grasp_v1/failure-watch/confirmation.json) · [Fitted probe](results/conditioned_grasp_v1/failure-watch/model.json)

On 32 new paired starts, 11 original grasps failed. Both monitors missed all 11, so observed lead time is unavailable. The tracker generated zero false alarms. The learned probe generated five across the 32 episodes, all on the 21 successful grasps (23.8% conditional). Episode AUROC was 0.584 for the tracker and 0.472 for the probe. The original plan held 21/32 and the selective return held 21/32. Unconditionally returning once to the calibrated view and replanning held 28/32, with eight gains and one loss against the original plan (paired exact two-sided *p* = 0.0391). Mean elapsed simulated time was 20.28 s on the original plan, 20.41 s with the learned gate and 19.26 s with the unconditional return, including timeouts. The score was the first sustained carry or the native deadline of 500 updates. These experiments measure the benefit of reacquiring a view after motion in this simulator and the limited diagnostic power of its early images. Physical trials were zero.
